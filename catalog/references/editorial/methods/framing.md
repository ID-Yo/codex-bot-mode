# Narrative and Framing Frameworks

Frameworks for shaping source-grounded information into compelling narrative angles.

## General Grounding & Count Rules
- **No Forced Minimum Counts**: Target numbers in method titles (e.g. 5 Lessons, 3 Tips, 3 Beliefs) are upper guidelines. If the source material supports only 2 or 3 verified points, do NOT invent or pad additional items to meet a quota. State only what is verified by the source.
- **Evidence Traceability**: Every lesson, tip, misconception, or step must trace to provided project evidence.

---

## Method: Problem-Agitate-Solve (PAS)
- **Purpose**: Highlight a real problem faced by the audience, examine why standard approaches fall short, and present a validated solution.
- **Required Inputs**: Verified problem or friction point, consequence of inaction, validated solution.
- **Structure**:
  1. **Problem**: Clear, concrete statement of a challenge or friction point.
  2. **Agitation**: The cost, risk, or frustration of leaving the problem unresolved.
  3. **Solution**: Actionable, source-grounded resolution or methodology.
- **Fallback**: If agitation points lack verified impact data, present the problem and solution directly without speculative agitation.

---

## Method: Misconception vs. Reality
- **Purpose**: Debunk a common industry myth or misleading intuition using factual evidence.
- **Required Inputs**: Common myth, verified counter-fact or data from sources.
- **Structure**:
  1. **Myth**: Popular assumption or widespread misunderstanding.
  2. **Reality**: Verified fact or counterintuitive truth from sources.
  3. **Why It Matters**: Practical impact on engineering, operational, or business decisions.
  4. **Next Step**: Recommended action based on the reality.
- **Fallback**: If the counter-evidence is ambiguous, present both viewpoints as an open evaluation.

---

## Method: Lessons Learned (e.g. 5 Lessons)
- **Purpose**: Synthesize concrete operational rules from an actual project milestone, retrospective, or incident report.
- **Required Inputs**: Verified retrospective facts, dates, outcomes, or incident postmortem.
- **Structure**:
  1. **Context**: Brief background on the initiative or milestone.
  2. **Lessons**: 1 to 5 numbered rules. Each stated as an assertive heading, followed by 1 to 2 sentences explaining the evidence and rationale.
  3. **Conclusion**: Single summary takeaway.
- **Constraint**: If sources contain only 2 or 3 genuine lessons, deliver those 2 or 3. Never invent filler lessons.
- **Fallback**: Fall back to 3 Actionable Tips if narrative context is limited.

---

## Method: Actionable Tips (e.g. 3 Tips)
- **Purpose**: Deliver immediate tactical recommendations for specific practitioner challenges.
- **Required Inputs**: Verified tactical domain guidelines or practical instructions.
- **Structure**:
  1. **Hook**: Direct opening identifying who the tips are for and what outcome they achieve.
  2. **Numbered Tips**: 1 to 5 concrete steps with actionable constraints.
  3. **Implementation Prompt**: Concluding question or prompt for the reader.
- **Constraint**: Omit tips that lack clear factual backing.
- **Fallback**: Single focused operational rule.

---

## Method: Limiting Beliefs (e.g. 3 Limiting Beliefs)
- **Purpose**: Identify false assumptions or perceived constraints holding teams back, reframing them with verified capabilities.
- **Required Inputs**: Target audience mindset, verified capabilities or architectural solutions disproving the constraint.
- **Structure**:
  1. **Hook**: Name the barrier or perceived constraint.
  2. **Belief vs. Reframing**: For each belief:
     - The limiting belief (what people tell themselves).
     - The evidence-backed reframing (why it is technically or practically possible).
     - The operational shift (how to act differently).
  3. **Conclusion**: Practical path forward.
- **Constraint**: Do not manufacture fictional psychological traits; focus on established technical or process misconceptions.
- **Fallback**: Misconception vs. Reality if only a single belief is supported.

---

## Method: Transformation Story (X Days Ago)
- **Purpose**: Structured before-and-after narrative tracking verified progress over time.
- **Required Inputs**: Baseline state, intervention/milestones with dates, verified current state.
- **Structure**:
  1. **Baseline**: Concrete description of starting friction or baseline metrics.
  2. **The Turn**: The decision, architectural shift, or method applied.
  3. **Outcome**: Measured, verified current status with timeline.
  4. **The Takeaway**: Generalizable lesson for the reader.
- **Constraints**: Dates, timelines, and metrics must be factually accurate. Never fabricate founder stories or unverified results.
- **Fallback**: Standard project retrospective if personal timeline is absent.

---

## Method: Engaging Questions (e.g. 5 Questions)
- **Purpose**: Spark informed audience reflection, community discussion, or polls on industry dilemmas.
- **Required Inputs**: Core topic, trade-offs, or tensions present in source materials.
- **Structure**:
  1. **Context Hook**: 1 to 2 sentences establishing the relevance of the topic.
  2. **Questions**: 3 to 5 open-ended, thought-provoking questions exploring trade-offs.
  3. **Discussion Invitation**: Clear prompt for comments or replies.
- **Constraints**: Avoid rhetorical traps or leading questions with obvious answers.
- **Fallback**: Single focused discussion question at the end of a short summary.
