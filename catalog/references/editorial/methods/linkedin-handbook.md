# LinkedIn Editorial Handbook

Revision: 1.0. Reviewed: 2026-10-21. Method ID: `linkedin-editorial`.

## Method: LinkedIn Editorial

Use for a LinkedIn brief, draft, review or existing-post audit. The project brief,
brand voice, declared sources and publication approvals override these defaults.
In a team, deliver through the existing Manager/Writer/Reviewer workflow. In
standalone use, give the same evidence note and audit to the Manager or owner;
do not invent peer approval. Loading this handbook grants no publishing rights.

## Editorial principles

1. **Name the reader precisely.** Specify role, situation, relevant problem and
   what they already know. Write to that reader rather than to everyone.
2. **Give one post one purpose.** Select a primary objective and funnel stage:
   TOFU (awareness: help a new reader recognize a problem), MOFU (consideration:
   explain an approach or evidence), or BOFU (decision: clarify an offer and next
   step). These are planning labels, not a required mix or algorithm signal.
3. **Make the opening useful.** State a specific tension, lesson or outcome that
   the body actually delivers. Avoid empty suspense, exaggerated promises and
   unsupported superlatives. A strong opening need not contain a question.
4. **Use natural short paragraphs.** One idea per paragraph, readable transitions
   and concrete language. Remove filler and forced line-by-line theatrics. Follow
   the project's voice rather than a generic creator persona.
5. **Earn personal proof.** Trace each personal story, quotation, result and
   number to an authorized source, owner confirmation or documented artifact.
   Confirm who did what and when, baseline, scope and permission to disclose.
   Do not turn a team/client result into the owner's experience or invent an
   anecdote. Without proof, omit it or use an attributed, nonpersonal explanation.
6. **Choose the CTA from the objective.** For learning, offer a practical next
   step; for discussion, ask a relevant question; for consideration, link a useful
   resource; for qualified inquiries, offer an authorized contact/demo route.
   A complete educational post may need no CTA. Avoid engagement bait, repeated
   requests and implying a save, comment or DM guarantees a commercial result.
7. **Learn from your own comparable outliers.** Compare unusually strong and weak
   posts from the same account against its own relevant baseline. Check audience,
   topic, promotion, timing and available sample size before borrowing a pattern.
   Record competing explanations; an outlier is a hypothesis source, not proof
   that a hook or format caused the result.

## Experiments, not prescriptions

Treat each option below as a test only when it fits the brief. None is a
platform requirement, proven uplift or causal law of distribution.

| Variable | Possible test | Guardrail |
|---|---|---|
| Hook | Number-led, first-person "I", or direct statement opening | Use real numbers/experience only. No hook type is universally superior. |
| Length | A 1200-1800 character variant versus the account's comparable baseline | This is an experimental band, not an optimum or platform limit. Record how characters are counted; never pad useful copy. |
| Format | Text, relevant original image, or document/carousel | Choose for the reader's task. No assumed reach bonus; keep topic and purpose comparable. |
| Link placement | In-post link versus another approved placement | Preserve access to needed evidence and goal completion. No claim that external links are penalized or comments improve reach. |

Predeclare the question, one variable, comparison posts, metric, observation
window and decision rule. Keep other controllable factors similar and record
confounders. Organic sequential posts are observational comparisons, not a
randomized causal experiment. Repeat before adopting a default. Keep missing
measurements as unavailable; never manufacture performance data or sample wins.

## Measurement and interpretation

Use the account's actual analytics/export and an authorized outcome record.
LinkedIn's official help describes impressions as displays and members reached
as distinct viewers; these are different denominators. It lists saves, sends and
post-attributed profile activity. Analytics are estimates; field availability
must be checked for the actual account and post. Definitions are documented in
the dated source below, not evidence that a metric causes distribution.

LinkedIn counts the author's own views, social and link engagement, including
saves and sends, in content analytics. Raw totals can therefore contain creator
activity and must not automatically be described as audience-only response.
Record whether creator activity can actually be identified and isolated in the
available data. If it cannot, label the counts as including possible creator
activity; do not invent a deduction or promise a removable activity breakdown.

For editorial usefulness, inspect saves/sends and substantive audience response.
For interest, inspect post-attributed profile views where available, separately
from all-account views. For commercial intent, define a qualified DM or lead
before counting it, then track verified inquiries and eventual results with
attribution limits. A save, send, impression or profile view is not a lead or
revenue. Avoid copying private DM contents or identifying clients into reports.

Record raw counts, denominator, metric definition, account, post, capture date
and age since publication. A follower-based rate is `chosen events / follower
count at the stated time`; an impression-based rate is `chosen events /
impressions in the same window`. Label the chosen event set and any percentage
conversion explicitly. Neither is automatically a unique-person conversion rate.
Do not compare follower and impression rates as if interchangeable. For zero or
missing denominators, report unavailable, not an invented rate.

Compare posts at a matched age/window (for example, each post's first seven
days), on the same account and relevant topic/objective/format baseline. Separate
paid and organic observations. Record dates, follower-count timing, cohort size
and limitations; never compare a new post with another's lifetime totals. If no
comparable baseline exists, record that gap and propose data collection rather
than a confident verdict. Use the existing performance note for confirmed results.

## Drafting and review checklist

- [ ] Reader, one purpose, funnel stage and brand/brief constraints are explicit.
- [ ] Opening promises something useful that the body delivers; paragraphs read naturally.
- [ ] Every claim and personal detail has a source, attribution and disclosure permission; gaps are omitted or flagged.
- [ ] CTA or deliberate absence fits the objective; resource/contact destination is valid and authorized.
- [ ] Length, hook, format and link-placement choices are labeled conventions or hypotheses, not algorithm facts.
- [ ] Primary metric, denominator, window and own-account comparison are declared; no fabricated outcomes.
- [ ] Exact draft revision and evidence note are separate from publishable copy; review and owner publishing gates remain intact.

## Per-post audit template

Copy one record per post. Audit exact existing copy before proposing a rewrite;
audit authorization does not permit editing, scheduling or publishing it.

| Field | Record |
|---|---|
| Source / revision | Post URL or supplied artifact; exact copy revision/hash; brief link; account and review date; handbook revision |
| Audience | Reader role, situation, problem and awareness |
| Goal | One desired reader outcome; TOFU/MOFU/BOFU; authorized destination |
| Evidence | Claim-to-source map, personal-proof attribution/permission, gaps and confidence |
| Hook | Exact opening; promised value versus delivered value; hypothesis if tested |
| Format | Paragraph/layout review, format, length/counting rule, link placement; brief overrides |
| CTA | Exact action or intentional absence; alignment with goal and permission |
| Metric | Primary metric; actual counts/source or unavailable; denominator; capture date and matched window; own comparable baseline and confounders; whether creator activity can actually be identified/isolated, otherwise label possible creator activity without invented deductions; qualified outcome/attribution limits |
| Severity | Blocker: unsupported claim, fabricated story or permission breach; major: wrong audience/goal/CTA or invalid comparison; minor: clarity/layout; experiment: optional unproven tactic |
| Recommendation | Specific evidence-linked correction, priority and next step; one-variable experiment only if justified; retain/omit when evidence is missing |
| Disposition | APPROVE or REQUEST CHANGES for this revision only; unresolved evidence; separate owner publication approval |
